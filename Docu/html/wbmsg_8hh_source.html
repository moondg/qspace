7 The msgXXX family is inherited from SysV. The mq_XXX family was
8 created by POSIX. Both families are however part of POSIX.
9
10 The two families have somewhat different feature sets the most
11 important being that the SysV variant allows for the specification
12 of different message types. This provides for flexibility in the
13 way messages are processed and in assigning priorities. The POSIX
14 family in contrast always returns the oldest message with the
15 highest priority.
16
17 As a rule the POSIX family is the better choice unless there is
18 something about SysV that you really need to use. The POSIX
19 interface is also less cumbersome. An added bonus (on Linux) is
20 that the POSIX mqd_t type representing a queue identifier is
21 actually a file descriptor. This allows for the extremely
22 convenient use of POSIX queues with select, poll and similar
23 facilities.
24
25 Basicly, msgget, msgsnd, msgrcv are System V IPC, while mq_open,
26 mq_send, mq_receive are POSIX IPC.
27
28 POSIX IPC is designed after System V IPC. So a lot of old systems
29 support only System V IPC while new systems begin to support POSIX
30 IPC as well. And, because POSIX IPC can learn the advantages and
31 disadvantages from System V IPC, POSIX IPC may be designed and
32 implemented better. A notable difference is that
33 all the POSIX IPC interfaces are thread safe.
34
35 Comments: SysV interfaces are also thread safe. There would be no
36 point to them otherwise
37 Well, I didn't confirm it but just read it from
38 docs.oracle.com/cd/E19455-01/806-4750/6jdqdfltf/index.html that
39 SysV IPC may not thread safe: Unlike the System V IPC interfaces,
40 the POSIX IPC interfaces are all multithread safe
